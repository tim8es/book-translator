## Chapter 192: Chapter 192

Chapter Notes

Hermione wrote back promptly enough that I was able to pick up a gift for Ron before the day arrived. The party wouldn’t be until late afternoon, but as Draco and I shared a bath that morning, I couldn’t help but ruminate over the time that’d passed.

I’d never made a very big deal out of my birthdays. The majority of them had been sombre affairs anyway, especially when I hadn’t been sure I’d see the next one. But for my friends to still be celebrating, I realised it was the little moments that all added up to a *life.* Sure, the milestones weren’t to be dismissed, but just the small intimate moments. Draco and I racing through the air towards the Black Lake. The hours of Wizard’s Chess I’d played with Ron. Even here, now, with him leaning back against me, humming something quietly and tracing spirals onto my knee as I held him in the warm water.

The history books and biographies that would recount my life wouldn’t ever *really* do it justice. Because they’d all be focused on the Big Events. And while there was no denying those things *had happened,* they’d never see the breadth and scope of the *blessedly mundane.* *God,* how I cherished the little moments.

We didn’t dress up *too* fancy. It was a Weasley event, after all, not a Malfoy gala. But once we were stepping up to our Floo, I felt Draco’s anxiety fluttering around his chest like a bird in a cage again. It was too late now for me to fuck it out of him again, so I just caught his worried gaze with a smile and sent waves of *calm* down the soulbond. Calm and *pride,* because I was *so bloody proud* of him. Not just for doing this with me, but for *wanting* to do it. For his *own* sake. He didn’t just want to make me happy, he wanted access to every aspect of my life. To prove to himself that he *could.*

He offered me a shaky smile, and swallowed just before he leaned down to press a chaste kiss to my lips.

“It’ll be alright,” he said quietly, and seemed to believe it a bit more than before, and I smiled.

“Yeah.”

Parcel tucked under my arm, I stepped into the Floo and departed. Felt like fifty years since I hadn’t been at Draco’s side, and when the flames died and the Burrow appeared, a little pit of *nausea* dropped in my gut to feel the distance between us. But I was quickly assaulted with a round of welcomes as I stepped into the living room.

Ron and Hermione got to me first, leaping up off the couch to pull me into a hug which I happily returned. The Burrow was decked out in birthday decorations, including but not limited to a little enchanted animal parade marching around the perimeter of the ceiling leaving a trail of sparkling 19’s in their wake. There was almost too much detail to take in, but then I was being passed off to Ginny, to George, to Arthur and Molly and my throat went tight to see them all again. The whole cluster of gingers who’d given me my first example of what *family* should be.

Draco was taking his sweet time, though, and before I let Molly haul me off to the kitchen to forcibly feed me, I hesitated. Glanced back at the hearth, and sent a little *anguished* tug all the way back to our cottage far out in the countryside. For a heartbeat, it was met with an equally anguished tug of his *own,* like *come home, come back,* but then it wasn’t pulling *me.* It was pulling *him to me,* like a lifeline thrown out into the ocean, and not a moment later, the Floo relit. And my cheeks split into a grin.

He was just about as pale and nervous as he felt when he appeared in the hearth. Reproachful and hesitant as he took a careful step out onto the rug. And of course, Hermione was right there to pull him into her arms. Ron offered his usual slap on the shoulder, and even Ginny sidled up to give Draco a small smile, and it was then I remembered Molly at my elbow, waiting to cart me off.

“I-I hope you– I mean, Draco, he’s–” She eyed me in that knowing, *motherly* way as I tripped over my words, but altogether seemed more *amused* than admonishing.

“Harry, you know you’re like a son to me,” she began, taking both my cheeks to face me towards her. “And it doesn’t do anybody any good to dwell on the past, hm?” I nodded, cheeks pressing back against her hands as I smiled. “Good lad. Now run along and eat, I made far too much food.”

“Right. Thanks, Molly.” She mirrored my smile, eyes crinkling around the edges just before she pressed a kiss to my forehead and sent me off. I couldn’t help casting one last glance back at my entire heart, though, and he was awkwardly letting himself be led between couches and chairs, likely also towards the kitchen.

*Bloody hell,* there wasn’t a surface in the kitchen that *wasn’t* piled high with food. Molly hadn’t been exaggerating, there was enough to feed a fucking *army,* and it all smelled *heavenly.* *God, I missed her cooking.*

I happily helped myself to a small meat pie, and was eyeing the rest when I felt Draco’s dread take a *swooping*drop through him. And froze to attend the very confrontation he’d been bracing himself for.

“Draco, dear, welcome to our home.” Seemed like *everyone* paused to watch, and the love of my life was feeling *painfully* put on the spot, but he just waited, tense and bristling in anticipation, as the elder witch neatly slid into his space and *pulled him into a hug.*“Thank you for coming, Ron’s told us so much about you.”

“Oi, don’t make it sound like I go on about him. *Mione’s* the one keeping tabs on those two, not me.”

“I do not *keep tabs* on them! I… Like to stay informed on my friends, is that so terrible?”

A grin crept into my face to feel Draco’s abject *shock.* So many leagues beyond *surprise,* and I could’ve bloody *kissed* Molly for being so… So *Molly.*

She released him from a *long* hug to take him by the shoulders, and by the time she caught a glimpse of his face, her warm smile had melted into a frown.

“You alright, love? Looking a bit peaked, have you eaten today?”

I barked a laugh before I could swallow it, because *if only* she could feel how Draco felt in this moment. Just as his shock was about to give way to something much heavier, *Arthur* stepped up.

“We won’t bite, lad,” he chortled good-naturedly, and just like that, Draco’s dread was back. I hesitated back towards the living room to peer around the corner, and poor bloke was white as a *sheet.* “Go on, make yourself at home. Molly’s outdone herself cooking, and it’s *not poisoned.”*

*“Dad,”* Ginny interjected, and Arthur raised his hands.

“Alright, it was funnier in my head.”

Draco coughed an uncomfortable laugh, and it was then he caught my gaze. I didn’t have to pull him to my side this time when he quietly excused himself to join me in the kitchen. Hushed whispers filtered to us from the living room, but Draco’s shock had finally cracked, and through it was leaking such a *tsunami* of emotion that I just took his hand and dragged him further into the pantry for some privacy.

He was about two seconds away from breaking down when I finally pulled him into my arms.

*“Fuck,”* he cursed under his breath as he buried his face in my neck. *“Fuck, fuck, fuck!”*

“Shh, love. You’re alright. Told you it’d be alright.”

*How can they just forgive me? Like nothing happened, like Ginny never courted death, like their son is still alive…?*

A flash of the vision I’d had of Arthur’s attack breached through my mind before I could hold it in, and Draco coughed a sob into my neck. Wilted slightly in my arms as I tightened them around him.

*Draco, it’s not your fault. The ones responsible are either dead or paying for their crimes, you don’t have to bear this burden anymore.*

His grief was great, though, and I couldn’t deny it affected me. Such a litany of names, faces, *people* had paid the ultimate price for the war, and we were both complicit in our own ways. Still, if Morty had taught us anything, it was that *feeling this way* was the first step towards *not.* And it would probably get worse before it got better.

I’d bear it with him, all of it, of course. He finally wrapped his arms back around me, and we clung to each other, letting wave after bitter wave wash over us in the Weasley pantry. How much *worse* this would’ve been if we were both alone. If we’d never found each other, and finally in the absence of *distractions* we’d have had to go through the fallout of the trauma we’d endured *on our own.*

*I’m here, I’m right here, I love you,* I told him. How easy it was now to frame my internal monologue into a *dialogue,* to think of him not as *him* but *you.* Draco was written into the very *cells* of my *being,* and it was so much better than life before him. Without him.

*Love you, sorry, I’m so sorry,* he answered as he wept onto my shoulder. And before I could scold him for apologising, I recalled just how quick *I* was to apologise. So much I still felt like I had to make up for. Had never been *enough,* didn’t fight *hard* enough or *soon* enough, but if I could want him to forgive himself, I could… *Try* to do the same.

He sniffed hard, and gripped fists of my shirt against my back, and I felt my own throat tighten. A steady stream of *pull yourself together, not here,* and the like was chanting through his mind as he attempted to do just that, but I was content to hold him as long as he needed. Would even conceal us, or Apparate us away if he needed, Weasley wards be damned. But he stayed right there in my arms.

I *wanted* to be everything he needed. To supply every necessity, to fill every hole left in him. But I was just one man. And, as Morty had a *habit* of reminding me, *just a kid.* Living alone, getting married, graduating didn’t mean shit as far as *adulthood,* and it was one of the first lessons he taught us: *no one has it all figured out.* I tried to take that to heart, as well as the lesson of *grace* I should afford myself. We could do what Morty called *‘shadow work’* all fucking day, but at the end of it, I was grateful for the outside help. Meant I could lower the expectations on myself somewhat.

*This’ll definitely come up in the next session,* I realised, and Draco bit out a pitiful little laugh as I felt his fists relax.

*Fucking therapy.*

---

Once I’d finally pulled myself together, we rejoined the party. We ate and drank, and *will the surprises never end?* *Arthur Weasley* himself was encouraging me to *drink.*

*‘Nothing like a stiff drink to ease the nerves,’* he’d said. Like he could just *tell* I’d be more at ease with some alcohol in me. I was grateful, honestly. He was treating me… *Normally.* Albeit strange, given our history, but it was *far*better than any scenario my imagination had conjured.

Ron opened presents, and was predictably surprised to open Harry’s gift: an *Effingo,* a model-sized representation of whatever structure it was paired to that could rearrange furniture in the real version without all the heavy lifting. I had to give him props for this, because despite the assistive spells we’d been taught that could help with such a thing, *this* made the process much easier and straightforward, with the added benefit of being able to see the entire space from a top-down perspective.

By the time the sun was low in the Western sky, I was pleasantly warm and tipsy on wine and sitting in the kitchen. And it was then Harry approached Molly with a request I hadn’t even thought to make.

“M-Molly,” he began, and the witch in question paused collecting empty dishes to quirk an eyebrow at his uneasy manner. “I… I was just wondering, really, if it’d be possible and-and if you have the *time,* er…” He glanced at me, and I smiled. “We… Don’t really know how to use our kitchen.”

Her curiosity resolved into understanding as she set the dishes in her hands into the sink, twisted to lean back against the rim with a hand on her hip.

“You want to learn how to cook?”

Harry nodded eagerly. “Yes, *please.* I’m… I’m used to *microwaves* and *stoves* and *ovens,* and all this…” He gestured vaguely around him as I lifted my glass of wine back to my lips. “I just… *We’re*… Both at a loss.”

A hopeless sort of sound huffed out of him as he deflated slightly, and Molly chuckled.

“Of course, dear. Let me show you the basics, and we’ll get you on your feet, eh?”

Watching Molly teach Harry how to cook was… I didn’t know the word for it, but I was *enraptured.* I gleaned a good bit from just watching them, but most of the time, I wasn’t paying attention to the actual *lesson.* I was just watching him. Seeing the light in his eyes when he finally figured something out, when he found an answer for his questions, and watching the Weasley mother work was like… Again, words failed me. Perhaps I was a bit drunker than I thought, but it was *so sweet.*

*Precious,* as I watched her lead him through the steps of using a cooking cauldron. This bit should’ve been easy for me, given my penchant for potions and my supposed *inhuman sense of smell.*

Harry cast a dubious smirk back at me, and I grinned. Refilled my glass from the abandoned bottle and took another swig. And it was at that moment Hermione drifted up to my side.

“You never had this either, did you?” I glanced up at her, arms folded around her ribs as she watched the ongoings affectionately.

“No, I… Never had to.”

She hummed, snatched my glass out of my hand, and necked a few gulps. And I huffed an amused chuckle as I eased back in my chair. When she offered it back to me, I was awarded a grin.

“She’s taught me *loads.* Shit you don’t learn at Hogwarts, homemaking for the muggle-raised witch as it were.”

I said nothing, accepted the glass back and set it down on the work table to twist the stem between my fingers as I returned my attention to the ongoing cooking lesson.

“Do you ever miss it? Muggle life, I mean.”

Harry was *adorable.* Eager and curious and open as Molly led him through the steps of thawing meat. And Hermione inhaled deeply as she contemplated her response.

“Yes. Life was… *Much* easier before I got caught up in the magical world.” I finally managed to tear my attention back to her, but she was still watching the two of them, a softness to her features and a small smile on her lips. She cocked her head. “But I often wonder if it’s just the… The *particular* magical life I’ve lived that’s made it more complicated.”

Hermione twisted a half-grin down at me, and I grinned lazily back at her. *My friend.*

“‘S worth it, though, isn’t it?”

Her half-grin blossomed into a full one.

“Definitely.”
