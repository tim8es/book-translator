Unbidden, another image flashed across my mind: Draco on his back in his bed, shoulders curling forward as his head pressed back into the pillows and a guttural sort of whine escaping his parted lips. And the bulb in the centre of my ceiling burst, sending sparks and glass onto the floor as I was plunged into darkness.

*Christ, this is bad. This is really, very, very not good.*

I sat on the floor a moment more as my eyes adjusted to the moonlight streaming in through my window, and I was silently grateful it hadn’t been *that* glass that’d burst. But my damn heart still wouldn’t slow down.

A silent tally of all the wrongs we’d inflicted on each other compiled in my dizzy head, but *this* infraction, this… This *thing* he’d done to me, tipped the scales heavily in my favour. And I vowed I’d have my revenge.

As I rose back onto shaky legs to repair my light, a gleeful sort of energy buzzed in my chest even as a twist of unease sank in my gut. I wasn’t sure how I’d have my revenge, but if he truly thought he’d only made us *even,* he was dead wrong.

After all, I’d nearly been sorted Slytherin myself. And two could play at this game.
