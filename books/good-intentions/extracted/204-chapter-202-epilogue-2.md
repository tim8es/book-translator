There was a storm rolling in as Harry and I waited on a bench. Platform 9¾ hadn’t changed all that much in the decade or so since we’d been back here, which I took a measure of comfort in as he leaned his head against my shoulder. There were a host of other parents waiting for the train to return, milling around or sitting on similar benches, but all of our friends’ children were still too young for Hogwarts. So we sat in silence, watching the sky darken and fingers interlaced as we waited.

*Reckon he made any friends?*

I snorted.

*Our Teddy? Of course, have you met the kid?*

Harry hummed, nuzzled my shoulder a bit and pressed a sighing kiss to it. At the beginning of the year, Teddy had written us nearly every week to tell us all about being sorted Hufflepuff, what classes he liked and which ones he *didn’t,* all the questions he kept getting about us, but had slowly stopped writing. McGonagall assured us he was getting on fine, so we had to assume he’d just become so wrapped up in school life that he’d… *Forgotten* about us.

It was as painful to think as it was *refreshing* to know that he was creating a life for himself. Of course he’d never had any shortage of friends, but both Harry and I were *deathly* curious to hear from Teddy himself how he’d gotten on without Rose, Victoire, and Dominique. Hugo and Louis were still just babies, but to be welcomed into a brand-new world of people and places and *education…* The lad would surely have a tale to tell.

A group of parents started to gather at the far end of the platform, drawing Harry’s head up off my shoulder as we peered towards the crowd. And sure enough, there in the distance was the unmistakable billow of smoke only the Hogwarts Express could churn out, rising over the hills. We stood and approached the edge of the platform, hands gripping each others’ tightly in anticipation.

In some ways, it was the truest test of parenting to find out how one’s child would fare *without* us. More than that, though, we were just eager to see him again. How quiet our house had been all these months he’d been away at school. As the train itself finally appeared, a mere pinprick on the furthest end of the tracks, I couldn’t help but recall the adoption process.

Andromeda was getting on in years, and while she loved Teddy *to death,* at a certain point it just wasn’t in her abilities to keep up with him anymore. Harry and I had watched him blossom into a *very* energetic child, and the solution had seemed obvious to us. We’d take over for her, and she was *always* excited to babysit for us, but once our friends had started families of their own, it became like a little… *Community effort* for us all to raise our children together.

It was a childhood of *warmth* and *family* like neither Harry nor I had been granted. And were only too *pleased* to give Teddy. I’d never forget the day he assigned us *dada* and *papa* respectively, because even after all those years of *bliss* and *peace,* it felt like the last little shadows of our past had evaporated in that moment. A *new* baptism, and I’d never been so happy to trade *Draco Apollo Potter* for *papa.*

Harry glanced up at me, eyes alight with excitement to see Teddy again, but also *soft* as he partied my internal nostalgia. Smiled and leaned up to kiss me, and I couldn’t help but smile back against his lips. *Christ,* I’d never kiss him *enough.*

The train’s horn blaring is what broke his lips away from mine, and we watched it finally close in on the mouth of the station. The rest of the parents on the platform were now gathering at the edge, tittering animatedly amongst themselves and I couldn’t deny I was just as excited. Even though our souls had remained intact, here came the missing piece of our *heart* as the train pulled into the station slowly.

We were scanning windows just like every other pair of parents, though a metamorphmagus could be difficult to identify on *sight.* Still, I’d know those dimples *anywhere,* and when we didn’t immediately spot him, we decided to give the train some berth to allow for the outpouring of students.

The behemoth of a machine took its sweet time coming to a full stop, and even longer before the doors were let open, but Harry was replaying every return journey he’d ever taken on the Hogwarts Express. How it’d always felt like he was leaving *home* to return to *prison,* and how *grateful* he was that that’d never be Teddy’s experience. I couldn’t disagree, because *our* home was so much brighter and *warmer* than the manor had ever been. So, so much more love awaited him.

When the doors finally opened, Harry stood up onto the balls of his feet to scan the crowd of students that flooded out of them. Up and down the long platform, and though I didn’t need to stand any taller to see over heads, I did, too. And when I spotted a little head of green curls bobbing through the crowd, I started pulling on *instinct.*

*There.*

Harry and I wove through reuniting families toward one little green-haired boy who was sharing a goodbye hug with a few other boys, and when he finally turned and saw us, my heart swelled into my throat. And his cheeks spread into a *bright* smile.

“Teddy!” Harry released my hand to push through the last few people to Teddy’s side, dropping to a knee as he threw himself into Harry’s arms.

“Dad!”

Oh, *god,* I was such a sap nowadays. I dropped to my own knees to pull them both into my arms, pressing a kiss to that neon mop of hair and damn-near *sobbing* at the return of the most brilliant little boy I’d ever *met.*

“Papa,” he chuckled under my kiss, and twisted his head out from under mine. And my throat went tight to see the little embarrassed flush that rose to his cheeks, because if he was nearly too old for *kisses…*

“Hey, Teddy,” I said thickly, deciding instead to ruffle his hair. “Love the colour.”

He beamed at me as Harry finally released him with a sniff. Took his shoulders and *stared* up into his face.

“How was the ride?” he asked, passing a sleeve over his cheek. Teddy rolled his silver eyes.

*“Fine,* dad. Honestly, I was only gone for a hundred and ninety days.”

Harry scoffed wetly, and pulled him back into his arms.

“Too bloody long, you ask me.”

“Dad, I’ve got to get my *trunk,”* he started to complain, but I was way ahead of him.

“I’ve got it,” I volunteered as I rose back onto my feet and silently summoned the luggage to my knee. It *might’ve* knocked into a kid on its way to my side, but she was *fine.*

“Come on, let’s get you home, and you can tell us *everything.”*

Harry stood, scooping Teddy up as he went and propping him up on a hip, and if he was too old for *kisses,* then *certainly…*

*“Dad!”* I chuckled as Teddy predictably struggled, cheeks going *red.* “I can walk on my own! Put me down!”

“Nope,” Harry said simply, and turned back towards the barrier to King’s Cross. “I’ll throw you over my shoulder like a sack of potatoes if you keep wriggling.”

Teddy groaned, buried his face in the crook of Harry’s neck, but grudgingly allowed himself to be carried. And I followed with his trunk, snickering into a fist as his green head of hair slowly changed to a vibrant *pink.*

“So *embarrassing,”* he moaned quietly, but I could tell he was just as pleased to see us again as his little arms wrapped around Harry's neck. Our little family was *whole* again, and the pure *joy* that radiated out of me was answered in kind by Harry’s own until we were both subtly *glowing.* Not that any Muggles would be able to see it; we’d long since figured out how to Disillusion ourselves from the public eye.

Harry carried him all the way to the Apparition point behind a little food vendor, all the way to our property and across the garden to our front door. And all the while, the more questions Teddy answered, the more he volunteered his *own* stories and gradually uncurled from Harry’s shoulder to retell his adventures of his first year at Hogwarts with *great* enthusiasm.

Once inside, Harry finally set Teddy down and wandered, still listening, to the kitchen to prepare him a welcome-home snack. With just a passing thought of will, his trunk silently ascended to his room and began unpacking itself as I lowered myself onto an island chair beside our little pink-haired Hufflepuff.

“Herbology was definitely my favourite. D’you know there’s a plant that *screams* when it’s repotted? One kid in our year actually *fainted* when he heard it!” Teddy threw his head back cackling, and Harry and I shared a grin.

“I’m assuming he was *alright,* if you’re laughing about it now.”

He nodded, still laughing, as Harry turned and presented a plate of tarts, leaning over the island on his elbows as he watched Teddy reach for one. It was so *strange,* to be hearing about a *new* story beginning at Hogwarts, one that wasn’t our *own,* and though similar it was still… Different. The wheel of time turns on, and all that.

“How’d you like Quidditch?” Harry asked, eyes *burning* with curiosity as Teddy took a bite of tart. His laughter quelled, though, as his face bunched up in a *pinch.*

“I… Couldn’t quite get the hang of it. Bit too wobbly, I think.”

He munched silently on his tart as I reached out to smooth back a bit of hair that’d been standing up. Teddy didn’t even seem to notice.

“Oh, that’s alright. We can practise plenty this summer, and then next year you’ll be leaving the other kids in the *dust.”*

The kettle started to sing, and Harry swivelled to pour us tea just as Teddy turned a *grave* expression over at me. Cheeks bulging with his tart as he gave me this *sombre* fucking shake of his head, and I choked back a chuckle.

*Don’t be too disappointed, love. Let the kid figure out what he likes. He’s not you.*

Harry huffed a little sigh, but returned to us with two cups of steaming tea and a *bright* smile on his face.

“Go on, tell us more. Did anyone get set on fire? Transfigured into a *ferret?”*

An indignant scoff coughed out of me as I reached for my tea, and Harry sent me this *wicked* little smirk that’d *definitely* just earned him a bit of torture later tonight, but Teddy drew my attention back to himself as he reached for his own tea. And I only sipped mine silently.

“Mm, no, not that. One kid *did* fly headfirst into the Potions classroom window, you should’ve heard the *smack!”*

As Teddy sent himself cackling again, I quirked an eyebrow.

*Quite the little schadenfreude we have on our hands, don’t we?*

*Reckon he gets it from his mum,* Harry replied fondly, and it was then his suggestion of introducing Teddy to his birth parents resurfaced in our memories. He’d made it through his first year of school, he was the same age now as Harry had been when he’d first glimpsed his parents in the Mirror of Erised. And though he’d *maybe* overused it, with the stone, we could properly supervise Teddy’s use.

There was no harm in letting Tonks and Lupin get a good look at who their son was becoming.

It grit my heart a bit, thinking about the pair, because Teddy was *brilliant.* Hilarious and spontaneous and outgoing, never stayed down too long when he came against obstacles, and *fucking hell,* I loved him so much it *hurt.* But even with us to care for and raise him, I knew from Harry’s own experiences what a deep, intrinsic *emptiness* parents left behind. Especially in one so young.

I didn’t feel like we *deserved* a kid as brilliant as him in our lives. Didn’t feel *jealous* at the thought of him meeting his real mum and dad, only… In our perfect little life we’d created for ourselves, I was *loathe* to admit there was still any *sorrow* to be felt. Wanted nothing but sunny days and smiles and laughter *forever,* but… Harry was right. Teddy was old enough now, and he deserved it just as much as his parents did.

“Can I go see the animals now?” Teddy asked, interrupting our mutual musing as he wiped crumbs from his mouth. And Harry smiled.

“Of course. Mind, Gregor’s been in a bit of a *mood* lately, so watch he doesn’t headbutt you.”

“Oh, I can take *Gregor,”* Teddy scoffed as he dropped out of his seat. “I’ll just Transfigure him into a teapot!”

He brandished his wand with a *huge* grin, and went sprinting through the house to the back door, chanting all the animals’ names as he went. And I watched with amused *affection,* leaning back in my chair with my mug of tea in one hand as he tore through the hallway.

“Bloody *menace,”* I scoffed into my mug as I took a sip, and Harry chuckled.

“Reminds me of someone I know.”

Saoirse announced her presence with a chirp just then, as if making sure the *menace* was gone, and came bounding up onto the table to sniff at a tart. Harry broke off a piece for her and set it aside, sliding the rest of the plate well away as he turned to lean back against the island. Peering through the kitchen window towards the little bundle of energy bounding back through the grass toward the chicken coop.

*It’s so nice to have him home,* he thought, so warm and soupy and *gooey* with affection that I just *had* to be near him. Stood from the island, set my mug down and rounded it to take him into my arms.

*Love my family so much.* Harry sighed contently and eased back against me, hands finding my banded arms around him as we watched the distant figure pry open the coop gate.

We just stood there, soaking in each others’ warmth and *love* for so long an old, familiar emotion swelled in me. This was what it’d all been for. All the death and the violence, the trauma and the *mistakes,* the scraping for survival. All so we could have *this.* This perfect bliss, this family, this *safety.* It’d all been so, so perfectly worth it.

And we’d both have done it all over again.
