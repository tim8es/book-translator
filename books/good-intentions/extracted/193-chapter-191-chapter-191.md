We ended up dining out… Quite often. We wouldn’t run out of money for *decades,* if at all, but my imagined scenario of us cooking together seemed to slip further and further out of our grasp as we realised this gap in our education.

And just like that, two whole months had flown by. I was sat at the kitchen island, sipping from a cooling mug of tea and reading a book about water dragons when a loud *BANG* followed by an outraged *screech* shook me from my reverie. Harry leapt to action as I straightened in my seat, and was surprised to learn the *Weasley’s owl* had full-on collided with our living room bay window.

*Is it alright?* I wondered, leaning back in my chair to try to peer around the corner (unsuccessfully).

*Just bloody Errol, blind as a bat.*

I huffed a nose-laugh and returned to my book, content to glean the letter’s contents through him. The bird in question gave another loud, offended-sounding *shriek* and the sound of wings told me it’d left. And as Harry sat on the window’s bench, the words before me blurred into the handwritten script of the letter in his hand.

*‘Hiya Harry,*

I abandoned my mug for his hand, lacing my fingers with his as I realised my palms had gone damp. A warm blanket of *calm* washed over me, settling like a weight over my racing heart as I tried to breathe. Festering anxiety thrashed back against it, though, and I squeezed my eyes shut.

Objectively, I *knew* I’d see them at our wedding, that our meeting was an inevitability, but memories of my father’s treatment of Arthur, the consequences both he and *Ginny* had paid for my father’s actions, the particular death that had found my bitch of an aunt all swirled around my heart until I was *sure* I’d either throw up or pass out, but then–

A hand on my cheek, gently turning my head. A mouth against my forehead and a nose in my hair, breathing deeply into the fibres. Harry sent a silent instruction to my lungs to follow along, and then we were breathing in unison. My head was light, but I held onto his hand, onto the sensation of his skin against mine, the warmth against my scalp like a *lifeline.*

*I didn’t even think about what going there might mean for you. I’m sorry. The fact that he invited us both so casually must mean he’s spoken to everyone, though. Still, we don’t have to go if you don’t want to.*

“No,” I croaked. “No, the l-last thing I want to do is keep you from your friends.”

Another swallow scraped down my throat as I slowly started to feel the chair beneath me again. The gravity of my own weight, the spatial awareness of my limbs. A kiss was pressed to my forehead.

*I could go alone?*

*“No,”* I emphasised, a bit more steadily as Harry finally leaned back to look at me again. And I forced myself to open my eyes, to face the concern in his. “I’ll… I’ll go.”

I knew he was probably right. That it’d be fine, because Ron wouldn’t have invited us without a word of caution if he didn’t think it’d go smoothly. Any guilt or shame I’d invent for myself would just be a product of my own imagination, and while it’d be at the very *best,* *enormously* uncomfortable, I just… I had to go. Not just for Harry, but for *myself.*

These people were a part of his life. His *surrogate parents,* and if I could face the *real deal,* I could handle this. There was no part of his life I didn’t want to be in.

Warmth bloomed in Harry’s chest, and in his eyes as he cracked a small smile. The hand still on my cheek invited my face down to touch my brow to his, and I let out a shaky exhale.

“It’ll be alright,” I echoed, tasting the words for their truth. And he nodded.

“Do fret about it, though, by all means.”

I scoffed.

We were given a mere two days before Ron’s party, and while he’d said not to bring anything, I was *sorely* tempted to. So I ended up owling Hermione secretly on the side to ask what their new apartment needed, if anything. Errol had reluctantly waited in the nearby woods for our response, and was given the task of delivering two instead of one. But he caught a good few field mice running around our land as payment before we had an answer.

I was out in the front garden the next morning, crouched in the grass beneath the Invisibility Cloak and waiting to see if any rabbits would emerge from our warren when I sensed Draco *fretting. Again.* Lingering in the bath and lost in thought about things he *literally* wasn’t responsible for. My eyes drifted shut as I honed in on his imagination of the way things would pan out, stepping into the Burrow and everyone leaping to aim their wands at him, spitting accusations, and I exhaled a heavy sigh.

*They wouldn’t dare,* I promised, even as he shook the scenario from his head. *No one would threaten my husband, least of all my friends.*

I felt his guilt for picturing such a thing, but the anxiety lingered.

*I know. Sorry, my… My imagination got away from me.* Beneath his dread for being treated like an *enemy* by those I considered *friends,* there was a deeper, more powerful fear of putting *me* in the position to *choose.* It dredged up an older worry, back from when we were first dating, that he’d have done *anything* to avoid forcing me to make a choice. Because he’d *known* I’d always choose my friends.

*I don’t have to choose. I’ve lost too much not to have everything I want now. And I already do.*

Emotion swelled in him, and I sent a little *tug* on the soulbond to my side. When he hesitated, shame for doubting me holding him in the bath, that tug changed to a *yank.* And of course, like always, he was helpless to obey.

Throwing the Invisibility Cloak off me, I inhaled the rich, earthy scent of the morning air as I tracked Draco’s dripping, reluctant approach through the cottage. As if I were about to *scold* him. A ridiculous notion, but it *did* inspire some ideas regarding… *Punishment.*

It wasn’t like I was really cross with him. My aim was decidedly leaning more towards *helping,* but as I recalled all the ways he’d ever wanted me to *hurt him…*A little shuddering tremor of anticipation fluttered down the bond from Draco as I heard the door open behind me. And it wasn’t like it *didn’t make sense.* I slowly twisted to face him, took in his glistening skin, dripping hair, avoidant gaze, and swelling prick in his lap as he sat on his heels. He’d never even *asked* anything of me (except to marry him) and I’d given him more than he’d ever expected. There was no reason for him to have ever *expected* I’d grow to like him. To crave his company, his conversation, his *body.* In the beginning, it’d all been so tentative. So dream-on-the-verge-of-waking fragile.

Draco’s little inhale escaped on a stuttering sigh, and he slowly, *gravely* nodded.

*“Yes.”*
