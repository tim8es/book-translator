# Style Guide

## Narration

- Point of view: alternating first-person POV, primarily Harry Potter and Draco Malfoy; POV can switch at chapter boundaries and at explicit scene/page breaks inside a chapter.
- Narrative distance: extremely close and immediate; internal reactions often interrupt the external action.
- Register: contemporary, highly colloquial British-inflected speech with frequent profanity; do not sanitize or elevate it in Russian.
- Typical sentence length and movement: mixed; long rushing sentences during panic/desire alternate with fragments, abrupt one-line thoughts, repetitions, ellipses, and rhetorical questions.
- Internal thought: source italics are semantically important. Preserve them in Markdown italics. Telepathic/soul-bond dialogue later in the book is also frequently italicized; preserve the distinction using context rather than converting it into ordinary spoken dialogue.
- Punctuation: preserve ellipses, fragments, interruptions, emphatic repetition, scene separators (`---`), and meaningful paragraph breaks. Russian dialogue may use «…» while retaining the source paragraph structure.

## Prose tendencies

- Expansive but fast-moving; scenes are driven by internal monologue, banter, physical detail, and emotional escalation.
- Lexically conversational rather than formal; occasional deliberately ornate/theatrical diction belongs especially to Draco.
- Highly expressive and emphatic; italics are extremely frequent and must not be flattened.
- Profanity is a voice feature and rhythmic device, not incidental noise. Preserve approximate force and frequency; choose Russian profanity by function rather than mechanically mapping every `fuck` to the same word.
- Humor is often self-deprecating, sexual, hyperbolic, or based on abrupt internal commentary. Preserve comedic timing.
- Explicit sexual content is direct, embodied, and emotionally tied to character voice. Translate fully and precisely without euphemizing, embellishing, or making it more clinical.
- Chapter Notes / author notes are meta-text and should remain recognizably separate from the narrative.

## Character voices

### Harry Potter

- Blunt, earnest, reactive, frequently self-deprecating.
- Often thinks in short colloquial bursts and swears when overwhelmed.
- Vulnerability shows through hesitations, stammering, and physical reactions; do not make him more articulate than the source.
- British everyday phrasing should become natural informal Russian, not pseudo-British literal calques.

### Draco Malfoy

- Sharper, more verbose and theatrical than Harry; capable of formal/aristocratic turns immediately beside crude profanity.
- Self-lacerating humor, dramatic hyperbole, possessiveness, and intense fixation are recurring features.
- Do not flatten his vocabulary into the same neutral register as Harry.

### Hermione Granger

- Precise, observant, direct, often the quickest to infer what others are avoiding.
- Keep her speech controlled even when emotionally involved.

### Ron Weasley

- Casual, earthy, teasing, blunt; warmth often appears through banter rather than polished emotional language.

### Narcissa Malfoy

- Generally more formal and restrained; maternal warmth develops against prior emotional distance.

## Recurring stylistic decisions

- Book title: `Good Intentions` → `Благие намерения`.
- Preserve canon spell names as transliterated incantations (e.g. `Expelliarmus` → `Экспеллиармус`) unless the text explicitly discusses their literal meaning.
- Use established Russian Harry Potter names/terms consistently as recorded in `glossary.md`.
- `fuck/fucking` is functional: render according to syntax and emotional force (`блядь`, `нахуй`, `ёбаный/гребаный`, `чертовски`, etc.) rather than one-to-one repetition, while preserving density and intensity.
- Slurs or crude self-descriptions are not to be sanitized; preserve their derogatory force when character/context requires it.
- Do not explain canon references or sexual terminology that the source leaves unexplained.
- Keep explicit emphasis around individual words/phrases where it changes rhythm or meaning.
- Preserve the relationship-intensity ladder instead of collapsing it: `be interested`, `like`, `fancy`, `crush`, `fall for`, and `love` are not automatically interchangeable. In particular, do not promote `interested / like / fancy / crush` to Russian `любить / влюблён` unless the source or immediate context actually carries that stronger meaning. Prefer context-appropriate forms such as `нравиться`, `интересоваться`, `запасть`, `сохнуть`, `испытывать влечение/симпатию`. Harry's gradual realization of Draco's feelings and of his own attraction must not be accelerated by stronger Russian wording.

## Ambiguities to preserve

- Do not convert uncertain attraction, realization, memory, or soul-bond impressions into certainty earlier than the narrator does.
- Preserve whether a thought is internal, spoken, inferred from body language, or later communicated through the bond.
- Preserve intentionally ambiguous pronoun/reference choices where Russian permits it; otherwise choose the least interpretive rendering supported by the scene.
